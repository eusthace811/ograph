Lane X: deploy ranking. A change on a healthy application should not outrank the root cause in
`ograph chains`.

GATE, written and committed before any code of this lane (2026-11-26)
=====================================================================
Diagnosis (dev data only: the distractor suite at --seed 2026, `distractor_eval.py check` on the
Parent; nothing of seed 9001 exists yet). chains rank 1 is right on 24 of 35 cases, 4 of 7 on
type b (deploy). In none of the 7 deploy cases does the planted deployment rank above the root:
it ranks 4th or 5th. Two mechanisms show:

  1. Known pairs cross dependencies. `DEPLOYMENT -> LOG_ERROR` is a known pair with a 0.9 prior,
     and Settings::link applies it whatever application the log errors are on. A deployment on a
     healthy callee therefore links at 0.9 to its caller's errors, with no evidence that the
     deployed application itself degraded (its own severity is 0.25), and its chain scores
     0.957-0.964, 0.01-0.02 below the true root (0.973-0.979 in the three misses). Nothing but
     that small margin keeps the deploy off rank 1; a root with a weaker first link loses to it.
  2. Suffix bias. The three deploy misses rank a caller of the root first (alarm-api,
     dispatch-portal, booking-web), whose chain is a suffix of the root's own chain
     (root -> caller -> ... -> incident). Confidence is the geometric mean of the links, so
     dropping the root's own link (severity 0.72-0.92 on these faults) raises it. On RCAEval the
     roots saturate (severity 1.0) and the tie-break by breadth hides this; in the suite they do
     not. The same shape accounts for most of chains' 11 misses on the suite, of every type.

Change (planned, design settled on dev data only): a change counts as a root only as strongly as
the evidence that its own application degraded. A known pair holds within one application (and
for pairs that close at the incident); across a dependency a link scores on time and severity
like any other. If dev data supports it, the same principle on the ranking side: an application
whose anomaly another application's chain already explains (it lies on that chain, after an
anomaly of a callee that was still under way) does not outrank the application that explains
it. No service name, nothing suite- or RCAEval-specific. The behaviour sits behind a
`[correlation]` key so that `ograph replay --set key=value` can test it.
